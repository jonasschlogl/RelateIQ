The days get shorter, the evenings get colder, and suddenly everyone seems to be looking for someone to watch films with. Your quiet dating app lights up again. An ex texts "hey, how are you?" That's cuffing season.

It's a fun name for a real feeling. But it also raises a fair question: is this connection the start of something, or just a way to get through winter?

## What is cuffing season?

Cuffing season is the period from roughly October to February when single people are more interested in finding a partner, or at least someone to spend the colder months with. The name comes from being "cuffed", as in tied to one person.

There isn't much hard science on it, but surveys point the same way. A Coffee Meets Bagel survey found that about half of singles think more about dating during cuffing season, and four in ten are more likely to use dating apps then. In a YouGov poll, around one in five people said they'd date someone in winter mainly to avoid loneliness.

## Why winter makes us want a partner

Part of it is practical. You spend more time inside, the social calendar fills with couple-heavy events, and the holidays bring questions from family about who you're seeing.

Part of it is emotional. Darker days can make loneliness hit harder, and a warm, steady person at home feels especially good. None of that is wrong. Wanting comfort is human. It just helps to know when comfort is the main thing pulling you in.

## 5 questions to tell if it's real or just for winter

### 1. Would I like them in June?

Picture a summer evening, a festival, a beach. Is this still someone you'd want next to you? If the honest answer is "probably not", it's useful to know now.

### 2. Do we talk about anything beyond the next weekend?

Real connection usually reaches into small future plans, like a concert in spring or a trip next year. If every plan ends at New Year's Eve, notice that.

### 3. Do I know their intentions?

This is the big one. Ask. "I'm enjoying this a lot. Are you looking for something serious, or more for now?" Being upfront about what you want is called [clear coding](/blog/clear-coding-dating/), and it saves a lot of guesswork.

### 4. Is the attention steady, or only when it's convenient?

Late-night "you up?" messages and cancelled plans are not cuffing, they're [breadcrumbing](/blog/breadcrumbing/). A person who actually wants you makes plans and keeps them.

### 5. Am I choosing them, or choosing not to be alone?

Be honest with yourself. If you'd be just as happy with anyone kind, it may be loneliness talking. That's okay to admit, and it helps you decide what you really want.

## Can a cuffing season relationship last?

Absolutely. Plenty of long relationships started in winter. Spending quiet evenings together, cooking, talking and meeting each other's families over the holidays can actually help you get to know someone well. Our guide to [meeting your partner's parents](/blog/meeting-your-partners-parents/) can help if that step is coming up.

What matters is not the season, but whether you're both honest about what you're looking for, and whether the connection is still there when spring comes.
