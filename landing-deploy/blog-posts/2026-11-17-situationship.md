You see each other most weekends. You text every day. You've met a few of their friends. But if someone asked "are you two together?", neither of you would know what to say.

That's a situationship. It's one of the most searched dating words of the last few years, and for good reason. In a 2024 YouGov poll, half of Americans aged 18 to 34 said they'd been in one. This guide explains what a situationship is, how to tell if you're in one, and how to get clarity, whichever way you want it to go.

## What is a situationship?

A situationship is a romantic or sexual connection that hasn't been defined as a relationship. There may be real feelings, dates and closeness, but there's no clear agreement about what it is or where it's going.

| Situationship | Relationship |
|---|---|
| You avoid the "what are we?" talk | You've talked about what you are |
| Plans are mostly short-term | You make plans weeks or months ahead |
| You're not sure if it's exclusive | You've agreed on exclusivity (or not) |
| You haven't met many of their people | You're part of each other's lives |
| You feel unsure how to describe it | You can say "this is my partner" |

## 8 signs you're in a situationship

### 1. You've never had "the talk"

Months in, and you've still never discussed what this is.

### 2. Plans are always last-minute

"Are you free tonight?" is more common than "what are we doing next month?"

### 3. You don't know if you're exclusive

You assume, or hope, but you've never asked.

### 4. You haven't met their friends or family

Or you've met them in passing, but not as "my partner".

### 5. You don't post each other

Not because you're private people, but because it would raise questions.

### 6. The connection goes up and down

Close one week, distant the next, with no clear reason.

### 7. You avoid talking about the future

Holidays, next summer and big plans feel off-limits.

### 8. You feel anxious more than calm

You wonder where you stand, and replay messages for clues. Our guide on [relationship anxiety](/blog/relationship-anxiety/) can help if that's familiar.

## Why situationships are so common

There are a few reasons. Dating apps make it easy to keep options open. Many people are busy, unsure what they want, or afraid of getting hurt. Some genuinely prefer something casual. And because "the talk" feels risky, it's easier to let things drift.

None of that is wrong in itself. A situationship can be fine if both people want the same thing. It becomes painful when one person wants more and the other is happy with less.

> **Why it works this way**
> Uncertainty keeps us hooked. When we don't know how someone feels, our brain spends more time thinking about them, which can feel like strong attraction. A well-known study by Erin Whitchurch, Timothy Wilson and Daniel Gilbert found that people thought most about, and felt most attracted to, someone whose feelings for them were uncertain. That's partly why situationships can feel so intense, and so hard to leave.

## How to get clarity

### 1. Decide what you want first

Before you talk to them, be honest with yourself. Do you want a relationship? Are you happy with casual? Would you rather end it than stay in limbo?

### 2. Bring it up calmly, not in a crisis

Choose a relaxed moment, not right after a hurtful moment or late at night by text.

Try: "I really like spending time with you, and I'd love to know where you see this going. I'm looking for something more serious. What about you?"

This is [clear coding](/blog/clear-coding-dating/) in action: saying what you want, kindly and directly.

### 3. Listen to the answer, including the vague ones

"I don't know" or "let's not put a label on it" is an answer too. It may mean they're not ready, or not interested in more. Take it seriously rather than hoping it will change on its own.

### 4. Watch actions over the next few weeks

Words matter, but what they do afterwards matters more. Do they make plans, include you and show up consistently? Or does nothing change?

### 5. Be ready to walk away

If you want a relationship and they don't, staying usually means more waiting and more hurt. You can end it kindly.

Try: "I've realised I want something more committed, and I don't think we want the same thing. I've really enjoyed our time together, but I need to step back."

> **Example: the holiday question**
> Olivia had been seeing Jack for four months. When he asked what she was doing over the holidays, she realised she had no idea whether they'd see each other or meet each other's families. She told him: "Honestly, I'd love to spend some of the holidays with you, but I'm not sure what we are. Can we talk about that?" Jack admitted he'd been wondering too. They agreed to be exclusive, and the anxiety Olivia had felt for months started to fade.

## What most articles miss

Many articles treat situationships as a red flag. But sometimes they're just an early stage that nobody has named yet. The problem isn't the situationship itself. It's staying in one when it no longer matches what you want.

It also helps to separate a situationship from patterns like [breadcrumbing](/blog/breadcrumbing/), where someone gives just enough attention to keep you around, without any intention of more. In a healthy situationship, both people are honest when asked.

## Quick answers

### What is a situationship?

A romantic or sexual connection without a clear label or agreement about where it's going.

### How long do situationships last?

There's no set length. Some turn into relationships within weeks, others drift for months or years until someone asks for clarity.

### Can a situationship turn into a relationship?

Yes. It often happens when one person brings up what they want and the other wants the same.

### How do I ask "what are we" without scaring them?

Keep it calm and focused on you: say what you enjoy, what you're looking for, and ask what they want.

### Is a situationship the same as friends with benefits?

Not exactly. Friends with benefits usually agree it's casual. Situationships often include dates and feelings, but no clear agreement.

### Why do situationships hurt so much?

Because there are real feelings, but no clear commitment, and uncertainty keeps your mind busy.

### How do I end a situationship?

Be honest and kind, say what you want that this isn't giving you, and then give yourself some space from them.
