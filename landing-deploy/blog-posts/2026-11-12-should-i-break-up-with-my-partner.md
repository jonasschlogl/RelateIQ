Some days you're sure you want to leave. Other days you remember why you fell for them and can't imagine life without them. You've made mental lists, asked friends, maybe typed "should I break up with my partner" into Google at 2am.

If you feel torn, you're not weak or indecisive. You're facing one of the hardest decisions there is. This guide won't tell you what to do. It will help you think it through more clearly.

## Feeling torn is normal

Researchers Samantha Joel, Geoff MacDonald and Elizabeth Page-Gould asked people who were actively deciding whether to break up what was pulling them each way. They found 27 different reasons to stay and 23 reasons to leave. The most common reasons to stay were emotional closeness, what they'd already invested and a sense of obligation. The most common reasons to leave were problems with the partner's personality, a breach of trust and the partner pulling away.

The most striking result: about half of the people had strong reasons on both sides at the same time. Feeling torn isn't a sign you're doing it wrong. It's what this decision usually feels like.

## 10 questions to ask yourself

Take your time with these. Write the answers down if it helps. Notice which ones you avoid.

### 1. Do I feel safe with them?

This comes first. If you feel afraid of your partner, are being controlled, threatened or hurt, the question isn't about compatibility. It's about your safety. Skip to the safety note at the end.

### 2. Do I still respect them?

Relationship researcher John Gottman found that contempt, things like eye-rolling, mocking and talking down to each other, was the single biggest predictor of divorce in his studies. Disagreements are normal. Losing respect is much harder to come back from.

### 3. Is this a bad season or a bad relationship?

A new baby, a job loss, illness or the holidays can make any relationship feel worse for a while. Ask yourself: was it good before this period? Would it likely be good again when this passes?

### 4. Have we actually tried to fix it?

Not "I've hinted for months", but a clear, calm conversation about what's wrong and what you need. Many problems feel unsolvable only because they've never been said out loud. Our guide on [how to fix a relationship](/blog/how-to-fix-a-relationship/) is a good starting point.

### 5. Is the same fight coming back again and again?

Some couples argue about the same thing for years without it ending the relationship. The question is whether you can talk about it without it getting worse every time. Our article on [relationship problems that keep coming back](/blog/relationship-problems-that-keep-coming-back/) explains what usually sits underneath.

### 6. Do we want the same big things?

Children, where to live, marriage, how to spend money. Love is important, but big life goals that really clash rarely disappear on their own.

### 7. If nothing changed, would I be okay in five years?

Not the best-case version of your partner, not who they could be. The person they are right now. Imagine the same relationship five years from now and notice how your body reacts.

### 8. Am I staying for love, or mostly out of fear?

Fear of being alone, of starting over, of hurting them or of what people will say are all very human. But if fear is the main reason you're staying, it's worth being honest about that.

### 9. Does my partner make me feel more like myself, or less?

Healthy relationships usually help you grow. If you feel smaller, quieter or constantly on edge, pay attention to that. Our list of [green flags in a relationship](/blog/green-flags-in-a-relationship/) can help you compare.

### 10. What would I tell a friend in my situation?

We're often clearer about other people's lives than our own. Imagine your best friend described your relationship to you, word for word. What would you say to them?

## What most advice gets wrong

A lot of articles treat breaking up as a test you pass or fail. Count the red flags, add up the score, done. Real life is messier. You can love someone and still not be right together. You can have real problems and still have a relationship worth fighting for.

The goal isn't to find the "right" answer in one night. It's to make a decision you understand and can stand behind, whichever way it goes.

## If you decide to stay

Make it an active choice, not just avoiding the decision. Tell your partner what needs to change, and agree on something concrete. Consider couples counselling if you've been stuck for a long time. And if trust was broken, our guide on [how to rebuild trust after a fight](/blog/how-to-rebuild-trust-after-a-fight/) can help.

## If you decide to leave

Be clear and kind. Choose a private moment, say it directly and don't leave false hope. You don't owe a long list of every problem. Something simple like "I've thought about this for a long time, and I don't think we're right for each other anymore" is honest and respectful. Then lean on friends and family, and give yourself time to grieve, even if you were the one who left.

## Quick answers

### How do I know if it's just a rough patch?

Rough patches usually have a clear cause, like stress, a move or a busy period, and both of you still want to make it work. If the problems were there long before and nothing changes even after honest conversations, it may be more than a rough patch.

### Is it normal to doubt a long relationship?

Yes. Almost everyone in a long relationship has moments of doubt. What matters is how often, how strong, and whether the doubts are about specific problems you can work on.

### Should I break up before the holidays?

There's no perfect time. If you've already decided, waiting weeks just to get through the holidays can feel dishonest and draining for both of you. If you're still unsure, the holidays aren't a reason to rush.

## A note on safety

If your partner hurts you, threatens you, controls your money or who you see, or makes you feel afraid, please reach out to a local helpline or support service before you end the relationship. Leaving can be the riskiest moment, and support services can help you plan it safely.
