How to use the text tone checker

  1. Paste the text you are about to send, or tap one of the examples.
  2. Read how it is likely to come across: a risk level, what they may hear between the lines, and a five-point tone checklist.
  3. Copy a better version. With a free account you also see their likely reply, a second version and a tip for your exact situation.

It works for any personal message: a partner, a date, a friend, a family member. It answers in the language you write in.

Why texts get misread so often

In a set of experiments, psychologists Justin Kruger, Nicholas Epley and colleagues asked people to write short messages that were either serious or sarcastic. The writers expected their tone to be read correctly about 78% of the time. The readers got it right about 56% of the time, not much better than a coin flip.

The reason is simple: when you write, you hear your own voice in your head. The other person hears nothing. They fill the gap with their mood, your history and whatever they are worried about that day.

Even punctuation does work you did not plan. In a Binghamton University study, one-word text replies like "Okay." or "Sure." were rated as less sincere when they ended with a period than when they did not.

The point stays the same in each pair. What changes is how much the other person has to guess.

What the checker looks for

  • Absolutes like "always" and "never", which invite the other person to defend themselves instead of listening.
  • Short replies that can read as cold, annoyed or uninterested, even when you are just busy.
  • Sarcasm and jokes that only work if they can hear your voice.
  • Blame that starts with "you" when the real message is how you feel.
  • Vague alarm, like "we need to talk", that makes someone anxious for hours.

Quick answers

Is the text tone checker free?

Yes. You get one free check a day without an account. A free account adds their likely reply, a second version and a tip for your situation.

Is my message saved?

No. The text you paste is not stored. Only a usage counter is kept, so the free checks can be limited per day.

How is this different from Grammarly's tone detector?

Grammarly's tone detector is a general writing tool, used mostly for work and school. This checker is built for personal messages: it reads how a text may make the other person feel, and how they might react, not just whether it sounds formal or friendly.

Does it work in other languages?

Yes. Write in your language and the read and the new versions come back in the same language.
