Green flags in a relationship: 8 signs of a healthy partner

Red flags get all the attention. We're good at listing the things that should make us leave. But it's just as useful to know what a healthy relationship looks like, because the good signs are usually quiet. Nobody sends a dramatic text when things are working.

Here are eight green flags in a relationship, and why each one matters.

Let’s slow it down. What did you most want them to hear?

1. They tell you when they're upset

Not with the silent treatment, a sulk or a sharp comment later. They say, "I'm a bit upset about this. Can we talk?" Being able to name a feeling without attacking you is a sign of emotional safety. It means you don't have to guess.

2. They can say "I don't know yet"

Healthy partners don't have an instant answer for everything. "I need to think about it" or "I'm not sure how I feel" is honest, and it leaves room for a real conversation instead of a rushed one.

3. They stay curious when you disagree

The conversation doesn't turn into a trial. They ask, "What do you mean by that?" or "Help me understand." They want to know your side, even when they don't agree with it. Our guide to communication habits describes this as listening to understand, not to reply.

4. They celebrate your wins

Psychologist Shelly Gable and colleagues have studied what happens when one partner shares good news. Their research suggests that responding with active, enthusiastic interest is linked to a stronger, happier relationship. A good partner doesn't compete with your success, play it down or change the subject. They light up.

5. They ask before assuming

"Did you mean it that way?" "Is something bothering you?" Instead of building a story in their head and acting on it, they check. That habit alone prevents a lot of unnecessary fights.

6. They repair fast, not days later

Every couple argues. What matters is the repair. John and Julie Gottman's research found that successful couples make and accept "repair attempts": a joke at the right moment, a touch, "Can we start that over?" or a plain "I'm sorry, that came out wrong." A partner who repairs quickly doesn't leave the tension hanging for days. If you want to improve your own side of this, see how to apologize to your partner.

7. They respect "I need a minute"

When your heart is racing in the middle of an argument, you can't really listen. Gottman calls this flooding, and his guidance is to take a break of around twenty minutes to calm down and then come back. A green flag is a partner who respects that pause and, just as importantly, actually returns to the conversation. A break that never ends isn't a break. It's avoidance.

8. You feel calmer after talking to them

This one is easy to overlook. After a conversation with a healthy partner, even a hard one, you feel more settled, not more anxious or confused. You don't leave every talk with a knot in your stomach, replaying what you said. If that's missing, our guide to overthinking in a relationship might help you sort out whether it's your patterns, theirs or a bit of both.

Green flags aren't a scorecard

Nobody has all eight all the time. People have bad days, stressful weeks and old habits. What you're looking for is a pattern over time: do these things show up more often than not, and when they don't, can you talk about it?

It also helps to know that attachment styles shape how people show these behaviours. Someone who tends toward anxious or avoidant attachment can still be a loving, safe partner, especially when both of you understand your patterns. We explain this in our guide to attachment styles.

A note on safety

Controlling behaviour, constant monitoring, cruelty or making you feel afraid are not signs of passion or worry, and they aren't something a list of green flags can fix. If you feel unsafe in your relationship, please reach out to a local helpline or support service.
